# Compass — what it is and everything it does

## The moment everyone knows

It's 8 p.m. Bedtime is a battle, your kid is melting down, and you reach for your
phone for help. What you get back is fifty strangers with fifty opinions, an hour
of scrolling, and somehow *less* certainty than when you started. You wanted one
good answer. You got noise.

## What Compass is

Compass is a calm companion for parents of young kids — roughly ages 2 to 8.

You tell it what's going on in your own words. Instead of handing you a pile of
articles to sort through, it gives you **one small, concrete thing to try right
now.** Just the next step. Something you can actually do tonight.

And it does it the right way: it remembers your family, it checks its advice against
real research, and it's honest when it doesn't know. Here's everything inside.

---

## What's inside

### 1. It gets to know your family — by talking, not by forms

There's no sign-up wall and no questionnaire. You just have a short, warm
conversation — you can **type it or say it out loud** — and Compass learns the
essentials: your child's name, their age, their temperament, and the thing you're
actually struggling with. One question at a time, like a friend, never an interview.

### 2. Your one next step *(the heart of it)*

This is the core. You describe a real moment — *"bedtime is a battle"* — and Compass
gives you **one concrete thing to try**, made for *your* child. Not ten tips. One step.

What makes it special: it actually does the legwork before it answers. It looks at
what it knows about your child, pulls up real evidence, searches actual studies, and
*then* commits to a single suggestion — and you can open a "behind the scenes" view
to see exactly how it got there. Every answer ends with a note on **when to put the
screen away.**

### 3. Make sense of what you saw online

Saw a parenting "hack" on a reel? A screenshot of a tip? A long article you don't
have time to read? **Paste in the link, the image, or the clip** — Compass reads it,
checks it against trusted evidence, and turns the good part into one step that fits
your child. And if it's *not* real, helpful advice, it tells you so honestly instead
of dressing it up.

### 4. Help in the hard moment — just talk to it

When you're in the middle of a meltdown, you don't want to type. So you **tap once
and speak.** Compass talks back in a calm voice, and it steadies *you* first — because
you can't pour from an empty cup — then hands you one tiny thing to do or say right
now. One small step at a time. And if you ask it something real-world, it can quietly
**look it up on the live web** and tell you what actual sources say, instead of guessing.

### 5. Tell a bedtime story — together

This one's pure magic. You and Compass make up a bedtime story **out loud, together,**
while your child listens. There's no rigid back-and-forth — it feels like two people
riffing. If you're on a roll, it stays out of the way; when you trail off, it picks up
the thread. The hero and the world are shaped around **whatever your kid loves** — and
the screen gently lights up with each scene as the story unfolds.

### 6. Your week, already curated

Every week Compass hands you a small "drop" made for your child right now: **a study,
a tip, and an activity** — laid out like a little magazine, not a wall of links. The
discovery is done for you.

### 7. A gentle daily check-in

Once a day you can take a breath and mark how it went — *tough, okay, good, great* —
and name the small win worth keeping. **No points, no streaks, no grades.** It's not a
scoreboard; it's a moment of credit for showing up. And every check-in teaches Compass
what's actually working for your family, so the advice sharpens over time.

### 8. It remembers your family — and shows you everything

Compass keeps a memory of your family: the facts about your child, and the little
story of what you've tried and how it went. That's *why* the guidance gets more
personal the more you use it. And it's completely open — there's a page that shows you
**everything it has stored, in plain language,** and a single tap to add to it or
**erase all of it.** Your data, your call.

### 9. Both parents, one Compass

Parenting is a team sport. A second parent can **join the same family** with a simple
invite code, so you both see the same memory and get advice that's on the same page —
no one's left out of the loop.

## The idea at the heart of it

Every piece of advice comes with a note on **when to put the screen away.**

That's the whole philosophy. This isn't about more screen time or less — it's about
**technology with intention.** A tool that hands you back your evening instead of
stealing it.

## The takeaway

Compass doesn't replace your instincts as a parent. It gives you one good next step,
grounded in real evidence, remembers your family, and gives you the quiet confidence
that you're not figuring this out alone.

> **Evidence, not authority. Technology with intention. Privacy by design.**
